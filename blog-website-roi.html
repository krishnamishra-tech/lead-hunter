Website ROI: What to Tell a Skeptical Business Owner

Sales · 4 min read

"I've been running my business for 15 years without a website" is a common objection, and it's not wrong — the business has survived. The pitch isn't that they'll fail without one; it's that they're leaving specific, countable opportunities on the table. Here's how to make that concrete instead of abstract.

Talk in numbers they already know

Ask what one new customer is worth to them — a single sale, a single client, a single booking. Business owners usually know this number instantly, even if they've never written it down. Once you have it, the rest of the conversation is arithmetic they can follow: "if a website brings in even two extra customers a month, at [their number] each, that's [total] a month against a one-time cost of [price]."

Point to where the customers already are

Most local business owners underestimate how many people are searching for their category nearby and just not finding them. Framing it as "people are already looking for [service] in [area] — right now they're finding your competitor instead of you" reframes the website from an expense to a leak you're plugging.

Use their competitor as proof

If a competing business in the same category has a website and visibly more reviews or activity, that's a stronger argument than any statistic you could cite. It's a direct, local, real comparison — not a claim about the internet in general.

Address the real fear underneath

Often the actual hesitation isn't "does a website work," it's "will I pay for something and then not know how to use it, or have to keep paying someone forever." Answering that directly — how updates work, what ongoing cost looks like, who they call if something breaks — usually does more to close the deal than more ROI math.

Make the case with a real number, not a guess

Guessing at potential customer volume undercuts the pitch the moment the owner questions it. The ROI calculator lets you plug in their actual numbers — average customer value, expected monthly visitors — live, in front of them, so the math is theirs, not yours.
