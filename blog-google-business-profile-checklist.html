Google Business Profile Checklist: The Complete Guide

Local SEO · 6 min read

For most local businesses, the Google Business Profile matters more for local search visibility than the website itself — it's what shows up in the map pack, and it's often the first thing a potential customer sees. Here's what a complete one actually includes.

1. Claim and verify the listing

An unclaimed listing can still appear in search, but the business can't update it, respond to reviews, or trust the information is accurate. This is step zero — nothing else matters until it's done.

2. Get the primary category right

This is one of the biggest ranking factors and one of the most commonly wrong. "Restaurant" when the business is specifically a "North Indian Restaurant" or "Coffee Shop" is a missed opportunity — the more specific accurate category usually ranks better for the searches that actually convert.

3. Fill in hours completely

Including special hours for holidays. A customer who shows up to a closed business because the hours were wrong (or missing) doesn't come back.

4. Match NAP exactly everywhere

Name, Address, Phone — should be identical across the Business Profile, the website, and any directory listings. Inconsistencies (a slightly different phone number on the website vs. the listing) confuse both users and Google's local ranking algorithm.

5. Upload real photos — a lot of them

Listings with more photos consistently get more clicks and direction requests. Interior, exterior, product/service photos, and team photos all help — stock images don't, and Google has gotten better at recognizing them.

6. Build review volume and respond to every one

Both review count and response rate factor into ranking. Asking satisfied customers directly (a simple link sent after service) is more effective than passively waiting for reviews to accumulate.

7. Post regularly

Google Posts — updates, offers, events — are underused by most local businesses, which makes them a relatively easy differentiator. Even a monthly post keeps a profile looking active.

8. Write a complete business description

A clear, keyword-relevant description (services, area served, what makes the business different) helps both the algorithm and the person reading it decide to click through.

9. Enable messaging and Q&A

Both are visible engagement points — an unanswered question sitting on a profile for months looks neglected to anyone who reads it before contacting the business.

10. Link the website (and make sure it works)

A dead or slow link back to the website undoes a lot of the above. This is where the free website audit checker is worth running alongside the profile check.
