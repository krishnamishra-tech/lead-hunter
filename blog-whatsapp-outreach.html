The Cold WhatsApp Message That Actually Gets Replies

Outreach · 4 min read

Most cold outreach to small business owners fails for one reason: it reads like a template, because it is one. Here's what tends to work instead.

Skip the introduction paragraph

"Hi, I hope this message finds you well. My name is..." — the owner has already decided this is spam by the second sentence. Open with something specific to them: their business name, their location, or something you noticed (no website, no recent reviews, no photos).

Lead with the problem, not your service

Don't open with "I offer website design services." Open with what you noticed: "I noticed [Business Name] doesn't show up when I search for [service] in [area] — is that something you've been meaning to fix?" This puts them in the position of confirming a problem, not evaluating a pitch.

Ask a question, don't make a statement

Messages that end in a statement ("Let me know if you're interested") are easy to ignore. Messages that end in a direct, low-effort question ("Would it help to see what that could look like?") are easier to answer with a single word.

Keep it under 3 sentences

On WhatsApp specifically, long first messages read as effort mismatched to the relationship — you're a stranger, and a wall of text from a stranger triggers suspicion, not interest. Three short sentences outperform one long paragraph almost every time.

A structure that works

  • Line 1: Something specific and true about their business.
  • Line 2: The problem that fact implies, stated plainly.
  • Line 3: A low-effort question.

Example: "Hi, I came across [Business Name] while looking for [service] in [area]. Noticed you don't have a website yet — is that something you've considered, or has it not been a priority?"

Send at the right time

Mid-morning on a weekday (10–11 AM) tends to outperform evenings and weekends for small business owners who are checking messages between customers, not scrolling for entertainment.
