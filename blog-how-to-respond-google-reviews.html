How to Respond to Google Reviews (With Templates)

Reputation & SEO · 5 min read

Responding to reviews isn't just damage control — Google has said review responses are a factor in local ranking, and prospective customers read them to judge how a business handles problems, not just whether it has any. Here's how to approach each type.

Responding to positive reviews

It's tempting to skip these since nothing's "wrong," but a quick, specific thank-you (not a copy-pasted "thanks for your review!") signals an active, engaged business to anyone else reading. Mention something specific from the review if you can — it reads as genuine rather than templated.

Responding to negative reviews

The audience for a negative-review response isn't really the reviewer — it's everyone else who reads it afterward. A calm, non-defensive response that acknowledges the issue and offers to resolve it directly (phone, email, in person) does more for your reputation than a long public defense of what actually happened. Keep the details off the public thread; take it offline.

Avoid: arguing point-by-point in public, blaming the customer, or a response so generic it reads as automated. Do: apologize for the experience (not necessarily admit fault on every specific), invite them to reach out directly, and keep it short.

Responding to fake or clearly false reviews

You can flag a review as fake through Google Business Profile, but that process is slow and doesn't always result in removal. In the meantime, a calm public response — stating plainly that you have no record of this customer/transaction and inviting them to contact you — protects your reputation better than silence, without escalating into a public argument.

How fast should you respond?

Within 24–48 hours is a reasonable target, especially for negative reviews — a delayed response reads as indifference. For businesses managing dozens of reviews across locations, having ready templates to adapt (rather than writing from scratch each time) is what makes fast responses realistic.
